Stargazer

Stand anywhere on Earth at any hour and see the night sky as it would really look from there: the stars your eyes or instrument could pick out through that night’s light pollution, moonlight, twilight, haze and clouds.

Usage

  • Look around: drag the sky, or hold the arrow keys. A flick keeps turning and slows to a stop.
  • Walk: W A S D to move at any time, Shift to run; the ground carries you over its rises and falls, and water, rocks and trunks stop you. A click on the sky captures the mouse for first-person looking around the crosshair (then a click picks what is under the cross, Esc lets go); on touch screens a thumbstick is always there, pushed to the edge to run.
  • Headlamp: the lamp in the toolbar, or F, steps a red headlamp through broad (a wide flood at your feet), spot (a tight beam that reaches 20 to 30 metres), thrower (a narrow beam that carries a few hundred metres), floodlight (a bright, wide wash over everything around you) and low, then off. Red light keeps dark-adapted eyes as they are, so the sky does not change. Its settings are headlamp.modes in config.js.
  • Choose where to stand: a first visit starts on Mauna Kea, Hawaii, with the place dialog open (choose a place or Start here); the place is kept in the browser, so later visits start there. In the game, Places (bottom left; Other places at the top on phones) or M opens the dialog: the night Earth turned to where you stand. Drag to spin it; tap or click to zoom in level by level; the wheel, a pinch or the + and − buttons zoom too. Tap a spot to see its name and sky brightness, then Stargaze here. Pins mark certified dark-sky places. The search box takes any place name or coordinates (-24.63, -70.40 or 24.63 S 70.40 W), spins the globe there and lands you. Esc or the close button goes back to the sky.
  • Time: the timeline at the top right spans the night from 4 PM to 8 AM; its track shows daylight, twilight and full dark, a band marks the hours the Moon is up, and it says when full dark begins and ends. Its date opens the date, Tonight and Now. (Tonight picks the first full dark, after moonset when the Moon sets before dawn. Now follows the clock.)
  • Instrument: the hotbar along the bottom, or keys 1 to 5 (each shows the faintest star it reaches): naked eye, handheld 10×50 binoculars that sway in the hands, 25×100 binoculars on a tripod, an 80 mm refractor and a 200 mm Dobsonian, each seen through its own upright eyepiece field; + and − (or the wheel, or the control above the hotbar) change a telescope’s eyepiece. Famous objects appear as real photographs at their true size, loaded when you turn toward them and freed once well out of view, as bright and colorful as that sky and instrument allow. Tripod binoculars and telescopes are set up on flat, dry, open ground where you stand, and aimed by dragging or the arrow keys; walking steps away from them and pressing their number nearby steps back. R (or the telescope’s slot again) sights along a telescope’s red-dot finder. The first time in a visit an instrument needs a larger star set, an offer opens above the hotbar with its size, and goes when you look around or walk; the menu’s catalog list has every download, and says which cannot be had here.
  • Why the sky looks as it does: a banner under the heading names daylight, cloud, twilight, a bright Moon or city lights when they hide the stars, with one thing to do: jump to the hour it clears, full dark or moonset, or find a darker place.
  • Sky conditions: the toolbar’s sky (it shows the cloud cover). Live weather and haze from Open-Meteo and the light pollution atlas; move a slider to set your own, Use live to go back.
  • Sky guides: the rail down the left, each with its key: G constellations, P planets (each ringed; the list beside it says where to find each one), I star details on tap, V faint stars (marks every star in view that this sky hides); tapping the sky again closes the details. Constellations show only the parts of their figures whose stars this sky shows above the land, and a figure only while enough is left to recognize; pointing at one (with the mouse or the crosshair) lights it up and shows a card: its name, where it is, what it does tonight, and its neighbours; tapping it keeps the card, a neighbour turns you to it, and Esc lets it go.
  • Help: Help (bottom left) or ? opens a guide to the sky, the numbers (magnitudes, NELM and LM, mag/arcsec²), instruments, catalogs, the ways to play and the controls.
  • Star catalog: the sky line names the catalog in use (naked-eye HYG, full Tycho-2, deep Gaia) and where it stops; click it, Stars in the toolbar, or the menu, to download or switch catalogs at any time.
  • Ways to play: Menu (bottom left, or Esc) picks explore (the sky with its guides) or the field game (no guides; a logbook of targets to find in the real sky, weather and land, each logged by holding it in view for points; L opens the logbook), and assists: no trees, flat horizon, clear skies, steady optics (each a quarter off the game’s points). All are kept in the browser.
  • Draw: Draw in the toolbar (or C): click star to star to draw lines; Enter lifts the pen, Backspace undoes, Save names and keeps the figure in the sky. A figure that traces a real constellation is named for it, and in the field game scores once. Awards and your figures are in the menu.
  • Sound: Sound in the toolbar plays the place, quietly, by its water, its land, its region and the night: surf, a river, lapping water, frogs, crickets, the region’s night birds and animals in their season, and wind only once the ear could hear it (rustling leaves and grass first, then wind at the ear, then a gale’s howl).
  • Opening a certain way: add lat, lon, date, time, az, alt, instrument, mode, guides, faint, lamp, clouds, humidity, haze, brightness, ground, view=globe (opens the place dialog), zoom or exposure (1 is the eye’s; more is a photograph’s longer exposure, for a picture of the pen) to the address (see the workspace README for values).
  • Keyboard: W A S D walk, Shift runs, 1 to 5 pick a way of seeing, + and − a telescope’s eyepiece, R its finder, G P I V the guides, L the logbook, C draw, F the lamp, M other places; Tab reaches every control; Esc closes a panel or a star’s details, lets the mouse go, or else opens the menu; ? opens help. On the globe, arrows spin, + and − zoom, and Enter lands on the marked spot.

File map

  • index.html — page, controls, panels and every template.
  • styles.css — interface: the HUD (heading strip, place and sky, timeline, guides rail, hotbar, toolbar, banner, crosshair), panels, cards, the place dialog and its globe, labels.
  • main.js — the sky scene, looking around, state, panels and wiring.
  • components.js — panels and idle fading, the globe (drawn on its own canvas in the place dialog), search, the ground, the soundscape.
  • game.js — what is kept in the browser (way to play, assists, progress), each write backing up the value it replaces.
  • shaders.js — sky model (GLSL), sky dome, stars, Moon, globe, ground, water, guides.
  • utils.js — astronomy, time zones, the visibility model, geometry helpers.
  • api.js — star lists, atlas, weather, places, terrain, map tiles.
  • land.js — what covers the land and where its water lies, read from map tiles around the site.
  • snow.js — the snow on the ground on the date, from NASA’s MODIS snow maps.
  • mvt.js — a small reader for vector map tiles.
  • state.js — the store.
  • data.js — places, instruments, grounds, atlas zones, planets, guides, sounds, field targets, copy.
  • config.js — every tunable value.

Customizing

  • Data (data.js): add a dark-sky place to darkSkyPlaces, an instrument to instruments (aperture, magnification, transmission and a practical loss set its reach; its true field, mount, set-up time and eyepiece set how it looks and handles), or a kind of land to covers (a Poly Haven texture, its color from afar, what grows close by, its trees and how many to a hectare, sounds; rustle says how loudly it rustles in wind, and nightCalls with regions which animals call there; landCover maps MODIS classes to it, and osmCover in land.js maps OpenStreetMap’s).
  • Tuning (config.js): visibility holds the sky model (natural sky brightness, extinction terms, cloud dimming); stars sets how stars are drawn (sizes, color, twinkle, how instrument-only stars thin out); sky.display is the screen brightness of a dark sky; terrain sets the horizon’s reach; land sets where land cover and water are read from and how finely; walk sets walking and running speeds, mouse sensitivity and how often the land is rebuilt around the walker; headlamp sets the lamp’s candela, beam angles and tilt; pictures sets how bright and colorful the pictures of famous objects show, how far and how long out of view before one is freed, the seeing and the in-detail bonus; drawing sets how near a click takes a star, when a drawing counts as a constellation and its points; game sets what assists cost, how long a target is held and how near the center; optics sets the eyepiece circle, hand sway, how mounts ring and shake in wind, and where equipment can stand; globe.levels are the zoom steps; renderer.maxPixels caps the drawing buffer.
  • Asset files live beside the pen in assets/ and are rebuilt by the workspace tools: fetch-stars.mjs (star lists, names, constellation figures), build-maps.py (light pollution grid, night Earth at 8K for desktops and 4K for phones), fetch-assets.mjs (ground textures and models), build-trees.mjs with bake-trees.html (each scanned tree drawn from eight sides into assets/trees/, and with &bare its winter branches), build-sounds.py (sound loops), build-objects.py (pictures of famous objects). Star lists are fetched, never committed.

Dependencies

  • three 0.186.1 — rendering.
  • astronomy-engine 2.1.19 — positions of the sky, Sun, Moon and planets; rise, set and twilight.
  • gsap 3.15.0 — the fade-in, the globe’s flights and panel motion.

Accessibility and devices

Every control is a native button, switch, slider or input with a label, reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring. With reduced motion on, stars do not twinkle, flicks do not coast, clouds do not drift and transitions are near-instant. Built mobile-first for iPhone and Android in portrait and landscape, iPad and desktop; the deep star set is desktop only.
